01 PHILOSOPHY & EXPERIENCE

What does a good home feel like?

Most people don't start thinking about a building through council requirements or roof trusses. They start with a feeling: morning sun on the kitchen table, a quiet corner to read, or a space where the kids can run around while you cook without feeling on top of each other.

"I love seeing people enjoy a home because we got the sunlight right."

It’s about tracking where the morning light hits when you wake up, and making sure the main living areas catch natural warmth during cold winter afternoons.

"Generous space isn't about huge floor plans. It's about room to breathe."

Good proportion and sightlines can make a modest home feel wide open, without adding unneeded building costs or empty hall space.

"How can a house feel completely open to nature without feeling exposed to neighbours?"

Privacy is about positioning windows, courtyards, and entryways so you feel comfortable keeping the blinds open all day.

"True warmth is timber underfoot, good insulation, and physical ease."

A warm home is draft-free, quiet, easy to heat, and stays pleasant through changing seasons.

"I think about how people are actually going to use the space together."

Connecting living areas to the outdoor yard, or giving family members their own quiet nooks while staying within earshot.

04 PRACTICAL BUILDABILITY

Speaking the builder's language.

A great design is only as good as its execution on site. Having spent decades working alongside local trades, my technical working drawings are written for clarity, accuracy, and efficiency.

I aim to provide working drawings that are clear, practical, and accurate. Catching structural questions early on paper saves expensive delays on site later. Builders appreciate straightforward documentation, and homeowners benefit from a smooth build.

"Good drawings aren't about being overly complex. They're about making sure everyone on site knows exactly what we're building and why."
